# Orders

CLIMS records stock orders between stores: a district store's orders to NMS or JMS (below). Orders use UUIDs for the product, unit of measure, stores and users, and record the authenticated account as `ordered_by_id`.

| Method | Route                 | Permission    |
| ------ | --------------------- | ------------- |
| GET    | `/api/v1/orders`      | `orders:read` |
| GET    | `/api/v1/orders/{id}` | `orders:read` |

Either store can view the order. `orders:all_stores` extends the assigned operation permissions across stores. Lists use `limit` (default 50, maximum 100), `offset` (default 0) and an optional `status`.

The first API slice also had generic store-to-store requests (`POST /api/v1/orders`, `PATCH /api/v1/orders/{id}/fulfill`, statuses `pending` and `fulfilled`). No client used them, and migration 32 removed them with the `orders:create` and `orders:fulfill` permissions. Orders already recorded that way stay readable; no workflow step applies to them (409).

Migration 6 creates the store and geography tables and seeds the national JMS store. Before district staff can use orders, provision their region, district and store with the relevant DHIS2 UIDs, set their account's `store_id` to the store UUID, and assign orders permissions through RBAC. Store and assignment setup currently requires database provisioning. Use existing `condoms` and `unit_of_measures` UUIDs when placing orders.

The supplied legacy dataset has no order rows. The migration archives any rows from other deployments in `legacy_orders` since the old table did not record the product. The legacy seed importer targets that archive.

## Stockout reports and warehouse orders

Two separate flows replace the earlier hotspot alert orders (migration 22). Health facilities are not part of either.

### 1. A hotspot reports a stockout to its DCFPs

A hotspot manager, or a VHT on the hotspot's behalf, sends `POST /api/v1/orders/stockouts` with the hotspot, a `condom_category` of `male`, `female` or `both`, and an optional `message`, e.g. `{"hot_spot_id": "…", "condom_category": "male", "message": "Dispenser empty"}`. It names no product and no quantity, and **creates no order**; the report returns the category as `selection` and the message as `comment`. If the hotspot already has an open notification, the message is counted against it rather than raising a second one (200): its `repeat_count` rises, and naming another type widens its selection (Male then Female is Both). Its history keeps every message, and each repeat notifies the DCFPs again with its own message and reporter. CLIMS records a stockout report (`stockout_reports`) addressed to the store of the hotspot's district and sends that district's DCFPs an `order.stockout_reported` notification. The notification gives the hotspot's name, village, subcounty and district, its latest contact person and phone number, the category, who reported it and the message. Its resource is the hotspot. Other districts are not notified. A hotspot without a district cannot report (422) until its district is set.

`GET /api/v1/orders/stockouts` lists reports newest first: those sent to your district store, about your hotspot, or sent by you, including an open report your own repeat was added to (`orders:all_stores`: all). `hot_spot_id` narrows it to one hotspot, `district_id` to the hotspot's district and `status` to one or more statuses separated by commas (`awaiting_supply`, `awaiting_acknowledgement`, `partly_received`, `fulfilled` or `rejected`). `q` searches the hotspot's name, village, subcounty and district, ignoring case. Each report carries its hotspot's `district_id` and the pieces delivered and acknowledged per type (`delivered_male_pieces`, `delivered_female_pieces`, `received_male_pieces`, `received_female_pieces`). Lists use `limit` (default 50, maximum 100) and `offset`.

The report is kept so a retried request still notifies nobody twice, and so a report survives when the district has no DCFP yet. What to order in response is the DCFP's decision, made in flow 2.

### 1a. The district delivers and the hotspot manager acknowledges

The DCFP records each delivery against the notification with `POST /api/v1/orders/stockouts/{id}/deliveries` (`orders:supply_hotspots` at the notification's district store): one or more lines of a product of a selected type and a `quantity` in the product's catalogue unit, an optional `note`, and an optional `client_reference` so a retried send records the delivery once. Each line is converted to pieces with the product's `pieces_per_unit`, taken out of the store's available stock (409 when too little) and written to the inventory ledger as a `hotspot_delivery`. The hotspot's manager accounts are notified (`order.hotspot_delivery`); a contact person without an account is not.

The DCFP may name the delivery agent who carries it with `rider_id`. Only an active user with `orders:deliver` who works in the hotspot's district (their assigned store's district, or else their subcounty's) may be named; `GET /api/v1/orders/stockouts/{id}/riders` lists them, and anyone else is refused (422, `error.rider_id`). The agent is notified (`order.delivery_assigned`) and sees the deliveries named for them, newest first, with `GET /api/v1/orders/deliveries` (`orders:deliver`): the hotspot and its contact, the lines, and whether the hotspot has confirmed receipt. Migration 35 adds `hotspot_deliveries.rider_id`.

The hotspot's manager acknowledges what arrived with `POST /api/v1/orders/stockouts/{id}/acknowledgements`, giving the pieces actually received per delivery line (zero when nothing came). Only a user assigned to the hotspot can acknowledge, or, when the manager cannot, the delivery agent named for a delivery, for that delivery's lines alone: district and national users cannot complete fulfilment. Sending a line's count again is accepted once; a different count returns 409, since corrections must stay visible. The notification is **fulfilled** once every selected type has a positive acknowledgement, and the district is notified (`order.delivery_acknowledged`). Until then its status is `awaiting_supply`, `awaiting_acknowledgement` or `partly_received`. A fulfilled or rejected notification accepts no further deliveries (409), and the hotspot's next stockout opens a new one. `GET /api/v1/orders/stockouts/{id}` shows the deliveries, acknowledgements and history behind the status. [Reports](reports.md) count these records.

### Where stock went: the distribution ledger

`GET /api/v1/orders/hotspot-deliveries` (`orders:read`) lists what district stores delivered to hotspots, one row per delivery line, newest delivery first: the hotspot and where it is, the district store, the product, `quantity` in its unit and `pieces`, who recorded it (`sent_by`), the delivery agent (`rider`, `rider_phone`) and the manager's `acknowledged_pieces` and `acknowledged_at`. It covers the deliveries of the notifications you can see, as `GET /api/v1/orders/stockouts` does, so `orders:all_stores` sees every district's. Filter by `district_id` (the hotspot's district), `category` (`male` or `female`), `from` and `to` (delivery days, `YYYY-MM-DD` in Kampala time, both inclusive) and `q` (the hotspot, its village, subcounty or district, the product or the delivery agent, ignoring case). Lists use `limit` (default 50, maximum 100) and `offset`. `GET /api/v1/orders/hotspot-deliveries/summary` totals the same rows: `deliveries`, `hotspots`, `pieces`, `male_pieces`, `female_pieces`, `acknowledged_pieces` and `unacknowledged_lines`.

The order list and `GET /api/v1/orders/{id}` also say when an order left the warehouse and when the district received it (`dispatched_at`, `received_at`, from its history); a workflow step's reply does not carry them.

On the web, MOH's **Distribution** shows both levels, read-only: **To hotspots** is the ledger above with its totals (period, condom type, district menu and search, all on the server), and **From NMS and JMS** lists the warehouse orders that have left a warehouse (`?type=warehouse_request&status=dispatched,received`), on the way or received, including stock sent without an order. Each row opens the stockout request or the order.

### 1b. The district rejects a request it cannot supply

Before any stock has gone, the DCFP may reject the request instead: `POST /api/v1/orders/stockouts/{id}/rejection` with a `reason` (required, at most 1,000 characters; 422 on `error.reason` otherwise), under `orders:supply_hotspots` at the request's district store. The request closes with status `rejected`, keeping `rejected_at`, `rejected_by` and `rejection_reason`, and its history gains a `rejected` event. The hotspot's manager accounts, and whoever sent the report (a VHT, say), are notified with the reason (`order.stockout_rejected`, also pushed to the field app). Once a delivery is recorded the request can no longer be rejected (409); a fulfilled one cannot be rejected either (409). Rejecting a rejected request again changes nothing and returns it (200), so a retry is safe. A rejected request takes no deliveries, and the hotspot's next stockout opens a new request. Reports count it as raised but neither fulfilled nor pending. Migration 38 adds the columns.

On the web, **Stockouts** lists the district's requests with status tabs (Open, Awaiting supply, Awaiting receipt, Partly received, Fulfilled, Rejected, All). Each request opens its own page, `/dcfp/stockouts/{id}`. There the DCFP either enters how many of each product to send, in its catalogue unit (the total shows in pieces as they type), or chooses **Reject request…** and gives a reason; three common reasons fill the box with one tap. The send form and the rejection sit in the same place, so the request is answered one way or the other. The client uses a stable reference for retried sends. It cannot send to a closed request, reject once stock has gone, or act from a read-only workspace. The field app shows a rejected request with the district's reason and lets the hotspot report again.

A hotspot manager may report for their assigned hotspot (`users.hot_spot_id`). A VHT with `orders:stockout_district` may report for any **active** hotspot in the district they work in: their assigned store's district, or else their assigned subcounty's (`users.DistrictOfUser`). Reports for inactive hotspots or other districts return 403. `orders:all_stores` may report for any hotspot. Products come from the catalogue (`GET /api/v1/catalogue/products`).

### 2. The DCFP orders stock from NMS or JMS

1. The DCFP sends `POST /api/v1/orders/warehouse-requests` with `warehouse_store_id` (NMS or JMS, the national stores from `GET /api/v1/locations/stores`), `condom_category` (`male` or `female`) and `quantity`, e.g. 500 female condoms, and optionally a `comment`. The district names the category, not the product, and no unit (a body with `unit_of_measure_id` or `condom_id` is refused, 400): the order's `condom_id` and `unit_of_measure_id` stay null until the warehouse chooses the product, and the quantity is then counted in that product's catalogue unit. The order is for the DCFP's assigned district store; an account without one is refused (403), even with `orders:all_stores`. It starts `queued`, and the warehouse is notified.
2. Warehouse staff `review` the order, choosing the product to send (`condom_id`, an active product of the requested category; required at the first review, 422 otherwise), which gives the order that product's catalogue unit (422 on `error.condom_id` when the product has none), and optionally approving a smaller `quantity_approved` (the request keeps the original `quantity_needed`), `check-stock` (which reserves the approved quantity, or holds it `on_hold` until stock arrives, when they rerun `check-stock`) and `dispatch` it, optionally with a smaller `quantity_issued` and the transport: `waybill_number` (up to 100 characters), `vehicle_registration` (30), `driver_name` (200) and `driver_phone` (30), each optional, blank meaning not given. Dispatch takes batches first expiry first out and never takes expired stock; a batch with under one month left needs an `expiry_exception_reason` ([inventory](inventory.md#batches-and-expiry)). Each step's quantity is kept in the order's events, so the original request and any revision stay visible. A request held `on_hold` has nothing reserved, so a new `review` may switch it to another product of the same category, and to that product's unit.
3. Until dispatch, the warehouse may instead `reject` the order with a `reason` (required, at most 4,000 characters; 422 on `error.reason` otherwise). A rejection closes the order as `rejected`, keeps the reason in `rejection_reason`, and releases anything `check-stock` reserved. A rejected order takes no further step (409); the DCFP places a new order if the district still needs stock.
4. The district runs `receive` on the dispatched order with the counted `quantity_received`; a mismatch requires a `discrepancy_note`. Receipt credits the counted quantity to the district store.

Workflow actions use `POST /api/v1/orders/{id}/actions/{action}`: `review`, `check-stock`, `reject` and `dispatch` with `orders:process` at the warehouse, `receive` with `orders:receive` at the district store. Run `GET /api/v1/orders?status=on_hold` to review held orders, `?status=rejected` for rejections, and `GET /api/v1/orders/{id}/events` for audit history. `status` takes several statuses separated by commas (`?status=queued,reviewed,on_hold,confirmed`). `type=warehouse_request` lists districts' orders to NMS or JMS, including stock a warehouse sent without an order, and leaves out hotspot orders and alert orders from before stockouts became reports; `district_id` narrows to the district of the store the order is for, and `warehouse_store_id` to the warehouse it is placed with. `q` searches the name of the store the order is for, ignoring case. Filters only narrow what the user can already see.

### 3. The warehouse sends stock before the district orders

NMS and JMS may send stock to a district store that has not asked for it, e.g. a quarterly allocation or a district they know is running low. Warehouse staff with `orders:process` send `POST /api/v1/orders/district-dispatches` with `warehouse_store_id` (their own store; `orders:all_stores` may send from either), `district_store_id` and `lines`: up to 20, one per product, each a `condom_id` and a `quantity` counted in the product's catalogue unit (the body names no unit). An optional `note` (quoted to the district and kept as each order's `comment`) and the optional transport fields apply to every line.

Each line becomes an order created `dispatched` with `unrequested: true`, and `quantity_needed`, `quantity_approved` and `quantity_issued` all set to the line's quantity. Only free stock goes: on hand, less what district orders have reserved and less expired batches (409 otherwise). Stock leaves first expiry first out, and a batch with under one month left needs an `expiry_exception_reason`, as at dispatch. The lines go together or not at all; a failure's message starts `line N:`. The district's `orders:receive` holders get `order.shipment_dispatched`, which says the stock was sent without an order, and they `receive` each order as in step 4 above. A sent order cannot be rejected. The warehouse request report and the store summary's order counts and response times leave unrequested orders out, because nobody requested them; their quantity still counts as stock dispatched. Migration 34 adds the `unrequested` column.

### Alert orders raised before migration 22

Hotspot stockouts used to be orders that the DCFP sent to the hotspot with a rider (`dispatch`, `deliver`) or covered with a linked warehouse request (`request-replenishment`). Those three actions are gone. Alert orders already in the database remain readable as history, but no workflow step applies to them (409). A warehouse request linked to one carries on through review, dispatch and receipt as usual. Migration 22 does not convert them to reports.

### Notifications

Every step notifies people through the shared [notification service](notifications.md), so they appear in each person's central inbox (`GET /api/v1/notifications`) with a message, read state and a link to the hotspot or order. A store is notified through the people holding the permission that acts on the step there, not everyone assigned to it, and the person who acted is never notified of their own step.

| Type                            | Sent to                                                                         | Resource |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| `order.stockout_reported`       | `orders:replenish` at the hotspot's district store (DCFPs)                      | hotspot  |
| `order.hotspot_delivery`        | The hotspot's manager accounts                                                  | hotspot  |
| `order.stockout_rejected`       | The hotspot's manager accounts and whoever sent the report                      | hotspot  |
| `order.delivery_acknowledged`   | `orders:supply_hotspots` at the district store                                  | hotspot  |
| `order.delivery_assigned`       | The delivery agent named for the delivery                                       | hotspot  |
| `order.replenishment_requested` | `orders:process` at the warehouse                                               | order    |
| `order.replenishment_approved`  | `orders:replenish` at the district store                                        | order    |
| `order.replenishment_rejected`  | `orders:replenish` at the district store                                        | order    |
| `order.shipment_dispatched`     | `orders:receive` at the district store                                          | order    |
| `order.shipment_received`       | `orders:replenish` at the district store, and `orders:process` at the warehouse | order    |

`order.stockout_reported` and `order.stockout_rejected` also carry the report's details in `data`: `report_id`, `hotspot_id`, `hotspot`, `village`, `subcounty`, `district`, `place`, `contact_name`, `contact_phone`, `contact`, `condom_id`, `product`, `reporter` and `message`; a rejection adds `reason`. Empty details are empty strings. The warehouse steps carry `product`, `unit`, `quantity` (the step's own: ordered, approved, dispatched or counted), `quantity_ordered`, `quantity_dispatched`, `from_store`, `to_store`, `note` (the rejection reason or discrepancy note) and `transport` (e.g. "Waybill WB-104, vehicle UAX 123B") and `unrequested` (stock sent without an order, whose `note` is the warehouse's). `review` sends `order.replenishment_approved` with the product chosen and says when less than the order was approved; a rejection quotes its reason; a receipt with a discrepancy quotes the note to the warehouse. The type names keep their earlier spelling so existing notifications stay grouped with new ones. `check-stock` and orders outside the workflow notify nobody. The earlier orders-only inbox, `GET /api/v1/orders/notifications`, was removed in migration 32: no client used it.

### Permissions

Migration 21 adds `orders:stockout_district` and `hotspots:read_district`; migration 24 adds `orders:supply_hotspots` and `reports:read`. Migration 31 grants the warehouse and DCFP roles the order and inventory permissions below by default (the `nms`, `jms` and `dfcp` roles), and migration 38 adds the DCFP's `orders:supply_hotspots` and `reports:read`, which 31 left out, so a DCFP can send stock to hotspots or reject their requests; administrators can change any role's grants in Users & access, and seeds never take a grant away. As with every role, grant other permissions through the RBAC API:

| Who                  | Permissions                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hotspot manager      | `orders:read`, `orders:stockout`                                                                                                    |
| VHT (field app)      | `hotspots:read`, `hotspots:submit`, `hotspots:read_district`, `orders:stockout`, `orders:stockout_district`                         |
| DCFP                 | `orders:read`, `orders:replenish`, `orders:receive`, `orders:supply_hotspots`, `reports:read`, `inventory:read`, `inventory:manage` |
| Warehouse (NMS, JMS) | `orders:read`, `orders:process`, `inventory:read`, `inventory:manage`                                                               |

A VHT without `orders:read` can send reports but not list them; they can still open the details of a report they sent (`GET /api/v1/orders/stockouts/{id}`), and only those. Migration 32 removed the unused `orders:deliver`, `orders:create` and `orders:fulfill`, with their grants. Assign the corresponding hotspot or store to each user. Record verified opening balances per store, product and unit as receipts with `POST /api/v1/inventory/movements` before warehouses check stock (see [inventory](inventory.md)). Dispatch and receipt add their own entries to the inventory ledger.

These operations are exposed in Swagger.
